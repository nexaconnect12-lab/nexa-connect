# NexaConnect market readiness roadmap

Repository assessment as of 14 November 2026. This roadmap orders the existing work by release risk. The [next-iteration roadmap](Next-Iteration-Roadmap.md), [QR roadmap](QR-Ordering-Roadmap.md) and [product expansion roadmap](Product-Expansion-Roadmap.md) remain the detailed backlogs. The assessment uses checked-in source and runbooks plus the current refund work in the working tree. It does not certify a deployed environment or establish market demand.

## First product and customer

Test a single-location restaurant with a staffed Windows counter, a kitchen screen, reliable connectivity and a manager who closes the day. The initial proposition is one workflow from taking an order through kitchen fulfillment, tender, receipt, refund and an auditable close. THB and cash are the starting scope suggested by the current implementation. Add manual PromptPay and guest table QR to the supported offer after each passes on the target release. Manual PromptPay requires staff verification of payment. Confirm the launch country and business timezone explicitly. A Singapore business-date test does not determine the launch jurisdiction.

Before committing to the scope, observe a service period and a close with prospective operators. Ask which order changes and modifiers, discounts, tax and receipts, accounting exports, card tenders and outage behavior are essential. Record what would prevent adoption and what operators would pay for. A requirement essential to the chosen segment becomes a launch blocker or changes the segment.

## Delivery sequence

Roles below are proposed responsibilities. A small team can assign several to one person, with a named restaurant owner or manager signing off financial and operational results.

| Stage | Work and suggested owner | Exit gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Freeze a pilot candidate | Product confirms the branch, hardware, payment methods and essential menu/receipt requirements. Engineering reviews the open refund changes and migrations, freezes a clean revision, and binds CI results, service/schema versions, installer and profile to it. | Exact revision passes required remote checks. The candidate has a named owner, supported workflow list and explicit feature flags. |
| 2. Prove the restaurant workflow | Engineering and operators run a synthetic shift on the physical install. Cover setup, PIN lock/renewal, ordering, kitchen delivery, tender, paper receipts, restart/recovery, partial refund, drawer/shift close, manager approval, and next-business-day settlement. For QR, scan with iPhone Safari and Android Chrome through the intended HTTPS edge and complete staff acceptance and collection. | Cashier and manager sign off the installed release. Receipts, movement history and settlement ledger/CSV reconcile. An interrupted operation is verified under its original identity. Disabled features stay outside the supported offer. |
| 3. Prove hosted operation | Operations runs the same build on the intended hosting topology. Exercise TLS and OIDC, least privilege, tenant isolation, staff versus guest routing, expected peak load, dependency outage/restart, installer upgrade, backup and isolated full application restore. Route alerts to a named responder and rehearse a schema-compatible rollback or forward recovery. | Authenticated checkout and close work after restore. Measured restore time, data recovery point and service capacity meet written pilot targets. Support can detect, triage and recover a simulated incident using the runbook. |
| 4. Run a supervised merchant pilot | Product and operations train the cashier and manager, provision their menu and roles, and agree escalation and downtime procedures. Verify local requirements for receipts, taxes and refunds, privacy/retention, payment handling and merchant terms with qualified local reviewers before real transactions. Reconcile each business day with the merchant. | Proposed minimum of ten operating days including a peak period, all days reconciled, zero unresolved financial or tenant-isolation incidents, and explicit merchant sign-off. Confirm the support burden and willingness to pay before expanding. |
| 5. Release to a small cohort | Product publishes supported hardware, tenders and connectivity requirements, pricing and onboarding materials. Operations rehearses provisioning a second independent tenant, access revocation, update rollout and data export/offboarding. Name the go/no-go owner and use the pilot evidence to size support. | A second tenant can onboard without developer intervention, data isolation is proven, and release/support ownership is staffed. Expand in batches based on measured results. |

Stages 2 and 3 can run in parallel once the candidate is frozen. Capture defects against the affected release, then repeat the relevant installed and automated checks after a fix. Record evidence in the existing acceptance packs and runbooks, with a release record linking the results.

The demo also shows capabilities that require product decisions before design becomes implementation.

- **Modifiers, set menus and promotions** (13, 17, 22, 32). Start with one merchant-required example, such as a required size with a surcharge. Define server-side validation and pricing, accepted order snapshots, kitchen and receipt display, and inventory/refund consequences. Track this in the [product expansion roadmap](Product-Expansion-Roadmap.md#2b-promotions-and-discount-management).
- **Split bills and mixed tender** (1, 5, 31). Confirm demand for equal split, item assignment or mixed tender. Approve allocation and rounding, partial payment/retry, concurrent collection, original-tender refunds and day-close reconciliation before exposing controls.

PayNow, NETS and card need a launch-market and provider decision plus settlement, expiry and recovery evidence. Customer lookup, credit and vouchers need their own permissions and business rules. Track channel work in the [product expansion roadmap](Product-Expansion-Roadmap.md#3c-website-ordering-and-delivery-integration).

## Release decision

- **Financial integrity.** Original order, receipt, tender, refund and close totals agree. Exercise duplicate submissions, competing refunds, lost responses, authorization revocation and cross-tenant access against the candidate. Verify amount limits, cash custody, exactly one financial effect, an auditable outcome, and a supported path for unresolved work. Check inventory reservation/release and kitchen transitions on cancellation and restart. Any unexplained variance, duplicate financial effect, lost order or unauthorized access stops rollout.
- **Service continuity.** Measure checkout and order-to-kitchen latency during a representative rush, time to complete close, recovery after a terminal or dependency restart, and restore time/data loss. Agree thresholds with the pilot operator before the trial. A broken critical workflow or a failed restore stops rollout.
- **Usability and support.** Have operators complete common tasks independently. Track interventions, support tickets per shift, onboarding time and failure causes. Prioritize errors that can lead to double collection, a wrong refund, a missed kitchen ticket or an unclosed day before visual refinement.
- **Expansion.** Consider promotions/modifiers or better stock controls when discovery identifies a daily blocker. Move offline execution or card earlier only if the initial segment requires them and their business and support gates can be met. Defer kiosks, delivery integrations, biometrics and AI insights until merchant evidence justifies the added support surface.
